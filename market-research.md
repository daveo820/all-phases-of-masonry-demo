### Patterns the winners share
1. **City in the H1 and title** ("Paver Patio Contractor in Wilmington, NC").
2. **Process and base explained.** ENC and Thorpe both sell what is under the pavers: base, compaction, drainage.
3. **Project photos by category.** Every site leans on its own photos.
4. **FAQ blocks** on service pages.
5. **One CTA repeated:** "Get a quote", "Book your free design consultation".
6. **What they get wrong:** slow phones (LCP 7 to 17 s on three of four), no prices anywhere, reviews pushed far down or missing.

## 4. The opening
Nobody in the set shows a price. All Phases has something none of them can show: dozens of verified reviews with the job price attached ($400 sunken stone patio repair, $2,200 for two sidewalks through tree roots, $5,000 concrete patio). The demo's hero is a wall built from those real jobs. The repeated review theme is price certainty ("no changes to the estimate, no cost overruns"; "the price was exactly what we agreed on").

## 5. Pitch points
1. He has no website and no Google profile. Neighbors recommend him by name; searchers find only Angi.
2. Real prices from his own reviews, which competitors don't publish.
3. Light and fast: hand coded, self hosted fonts, no photo needed above the fold.
4. A Google Business Profile would be the natural next step (free to set up; not something we promise results from).

## 6. Risks and caveats
- Angi says no warranties and no emergency service. The demo claims neither.
- Licensing and insurance are not verified; the demo does not mention them.
- One 2024 review mentions concrete color picked without consulting the customer (still positive overall). Not quoted, but Davis should know it exists.
- Prices are what customers paid on specific jobs in 2023 to 2026, not a price list.
- No photos of his work are public that we could use; all photo spots are placeholders.
- Competitor scores are single runs and move between runs.
